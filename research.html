RESEARCH · LIS × WII SOFTWARE

Research archive

Exploring how library and information science can help document, organise, preserve, and communicate knowledge about Wii software and WiiWare.

RESEARCH AREAS

From playable software to documented knowledge.

01 · WIIWARE

WiiWare file research

Investigate individual WiiWare titles to understand their file structures, embedded data, development remnants, and modification possibilities.

Initial work is exploratory: observe, document, compare, and test before attempting to assign meaning to unknown structures.

02 · STRUCTURES

File structure analysis

Document directories, filenames, file sizes, extensions, hashes, offsets, magic values, binary patterns, and relationships between files.

A future file-structure viewer could make this evidence easier to inspect and preserve.

03 · PROVENANCE

Development remnants

Study whether software archives contain unused material, earlier-build remnants, shared development assets, or other residual data.

Observations should be separated from hypotheses, with evidence recorded for each conclusion.

04 · PRESERVATION

Digital preservation

Preservation is not only about keeping a title executable. It can also mean preserving the knowledge needed to identify, understand, document, and reproduce research about it.

LIS METHODOLOGY

Applying information organisation to reverse engineering.

Wii reverse engineering produces a lot of fragmented knowledge: filenames, observations, experiments, hypotheses, version differences, hashes, screenshots, and technical notes.

Library and information science provides useful ways to make that material findable and understandable over time.

01Metadata

Identify titles, versions, files, formats, dates, tools, and research context.

02Provenance

Track where evidence came from and distinguish original, modified, extracted, and generated material.

03Authority control

Use consistent names and identifiers for titles, formats, tools, and recurring structures.

04Technical documentation

Turn scattered observations into reproducible research notes and structured findings.

METHOD

Observation → hypothesis → experiment → result.

01Record the evidence

Preserve filenames, paths, sizes, hashes, offsets, screenshots, and other observations before interpretation.

02Form a hypothesis

Describe what a structure might represent while clearly marking the idea as uncertain.

03Test the hypothesis

Compare versions, inspect binaries, modify controlled copies, or reproduce a behaviour where appropriate.

04Document the result

Record what changed, what was learned, what remains unknown, and how another researcher could reproduce the observation.

LONG-TERM DIRECTION

A research archive for Wii software.

MarcViiew is the catalogue side of the idea. A future research archive can provide the deeper technical layer: per-title documentation, file inventories, format notes, experiments, modification records, and preservation metadata.
